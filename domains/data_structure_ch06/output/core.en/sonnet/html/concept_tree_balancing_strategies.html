Tree Balancing Strategies

A binary search tree (BST) supports search, insertion, and deletion in $O(h)$ time, where $h$ is the tree's height. For $n$ nodes, the ideal height is $\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor$, achieved by a perfectly balanced tree. However, inserting keys in sorted order degenerates a BST into a linked list with $h = n - 1$, collapsing all operations to $O(n)$. Tree balancing strategies are structural maintenance rules that bound $h$ to $O(\log n)$ regardless of insertion order.

Definition (Balance Factor). For a node $v$ in a binary tree, let $h_L(v)$ and $h_R(v)$ denote the heights of its left and right subtrees, respectively. The balance factor of $v$ is

$$\beta(v) = h_L(v) - h_R(v).$$

A tree is AVL-balanced if $|\beta(v)| \leq 1$ for every node $v$.

Worked Example. Consider inserting the sequence $1, 2, 3$ into an AVL tree. After inserting $1$ and $2$, the root has $\beta = -1$, which is acceptable. Inserting $3$ makes the root's balance factor $\beta = -2$, violating the AVL condition. A left rotation at the root resolves this: node $2$ becomes the new root, with $1$ as its left child and $3$ as its right child. The resulting tree has height $1$ and $\beta = 0$ at every node.

Rotations are the fundamental repair operation. A left rotation at node $x$ promotes $x$'s right child $y$ to $x$'s position, making $x$ the left child of $y$ and attaching $y$'s former left subtree as $x$'s right subtree. The symmetric operation is a right rotation. For doubly-imbalanced cases (left-right or right-left), a double rotation — two single rotations in sequence — restores balance.

Theorem (AVL Height Bound). An AVL tree with $n$ nodes satisfies $h \leq 1.44 \log_2(n + 2)$.

Proof sketch. Let $N(h)$ be the minimum number of nodes in an AVL tree of height $h$. The recurrence $N(h) = N(h-1) + N(h-2) + 1$ with $N(0)=1$, $N(1)=2$ grows like Fibonacci numbers, giving $N(h) \approx \phi^h / \sqrt{5}$, where $\phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$. Inverting yields $h \leq \log_\phi(n\sqrt{5}) \approx 1.44\log_2 n$. $\square$
