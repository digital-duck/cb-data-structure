Matrix Powers For Paths

Definition. Let $G$ be a directed graph on $n$ vertices, and let $A \in \{0,1\}^{n \times n}$ be its adjacency matrix, where $A_{ij} = 1$ if there is a directed edge from vertex $i$ to vertex $j$, and $0$ otherwise. For a non-negative integer $k$, the $(i,j)$-entry of the matrix power $A^k$ counts the number of distinct directed walks of length exactly $k$ from vertex $i$ to vertex $j$.

More precisely, $\bigl(A^k\bigr)_{ij} = \sum_{\text{walks } i \to j \text{ of length } k} 1$, where a walk of length $k$ is a sequence of vertices $v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_k$ with $v_0 = i$, $v_k = j$, and an edge from $v_{t}$ to $v_{t+1}$ for each $t$.

Worked Example. Consider the graph with vertices $\{1, 2, 3\}$ and edges $1\!\to\!2$, $2\!\to\!3$, $3\!\to\!1$, $1\!\to\!3$. Its adjacency matrix is

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Computing $A^2$:

$$A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The entry $\bigl(A^2\bigr)_{13} = 1$ confirms that there is exactly one walk of length 2 from vertex 1 to vertex 3, namely $1 \to 2 \to 3$. The entry $\bigl(A^2\bigr)_{11} = 1$ reflects the walk $1 \to 3 \to 1$.

Key Theorem. (Walk-Counting via Matrix Powers.) For any directed graph with adjacency matrix $A$, $\bigl(A^k\bigr)_{ij}$ equals the number of walks of length $k$ from $i$ to $j$.

Proof sketch. By induction on $k$. The base case $k=1$ holds by definition of $A$. Assuming the result for $k-1$, the number of walks of length $k$ from $i$ to $j$ is $\sum_\ell \bigl(A^{k-1}\bigr)_{i\ell} \cdot A_{\ell j}$, which is precisely the matrix product formula for $\bigl(A^k\bigr)_{ij}$. $\square$

from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[0,1,1],[0,0,1],[1,0,0]])
A2 = A**2
A3 = A**3
print("A^2 =", A2)
print("A^3 =", A3)
# Entry (0,2) of A^3: walks of length 3 from vertex 1 to vertex 3
print("Walks 1->3 of length 3:", A3[0, 2])
