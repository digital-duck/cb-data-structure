Concept Book: Absorb Operation

Data Structure Ch10 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What absorption achieves. The absorption operation is the pair of identities

$$a \lor (a \land b) = a, \qquad a \land (a \lor b) = a.$$

A term that is already implied by $a$ is absorbed and disappears. The identities are the natural endpoint because they tie together everything built earlier. Commutativity, associativity, and idempotence describe $\lor$ and $\land$ separately. Absorption is the only law that makes them interact. With it, the two operations become the join and meet of a lattice, and $a \land b = a$ becomes equivalent to $a \lor b = b$. Both say $a \le b$.

Proof sketch. Since $a \land b \le a$, the join of $a$ with something below it is $a$. The dual identity follows in the same way.

Worked example. Simplify $F = x\,y + x\,y\,z + x\,y\,\bar z$.

  • Factor: $x\,y\,(1 + z + \bar z)$.
  • Since $1 + z = 1$, this becomes $x\,y$.

    • Absorption does this in one step. The term $x\,y$ already contains every case where $x\,y\,z$ or $x\,y\,\bar z$ is true, so $F = x\,y$.

    Problem-solving use. To simplify any expression, look for a term that is a sub-condition of another term and delete the more specific one. This works as a pattern, so you don't need a truth table.

    Connections to application domains.

  • Digital logic. Each absorbed product term removes a gate. In Karnaugh-map reduction, a group covered by a larger group is exactly an absorbed term, so absorption is the reason minimal circuits are smaller.
  • Sets and databases. For sets, $A \cup (A \cap B) = A$. A query such as "customers in region $A$, or in region $A$ and with a purchase" reduces to "customers in region $A$." Query optimizers apply this rewrite to drop redundant conditions.
  • Probability. Events satisfy the same laws, so $P\big(A \cup (A \cap B)\big) = P(A)$. This avoids double-counting when you enumerate cases.
  • Order theory and lattices. Absorption is what lets you define the order $\le$ from the algebra alone. Divisibility lattices, subset lattices, and the "greater of / lesser of" structure on numbers ($\max$ and $\min$) all obey it.

    • Invitation. Pick one domain and test the law yourself. A good start is digital logic. Take a three-variable function from a truth table and write it as a sum of minterms. Then apply absorption repeatedly until no term can be removed. Count the gates before and after. That count shows how much work the two identities save.
